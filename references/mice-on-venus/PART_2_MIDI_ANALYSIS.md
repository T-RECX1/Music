# Mice on Venus — Part 2 MIDI Reconstruction Analysis

> Source: Tatum's uploaded MIDI reconstruction stems, analyzed as a compositional/arrangement study. This is not claimed to be an authoritative transcription of the released master.

## Source set

- `Mice_on_Venus_String_Pizzicato.mid`
- `Mice_on_Venus_Harp.mid`
- `Mice_on_Venus_Mallet.mid`
- `Mice_on_Venus_Lead.mid`
- `Mice_on_Venus_Strings.mid`
- `Mice_on_Venus_Background_Synth.mid`
- `Mice_on_venus_part_2_Copy.mid` — appears to be an earlier/composite working version; most of its note material overlaps the later separated stems and it ends earlier, so the named stems are treated as the primary arrangement source.

## Measured project facts

- Tempo: approximately **110 BPM**.
- Meter: **4/4**.
- Longest separated stems extend to about **204 beats = 51 bars**, roughly **1:51** at 110 BPM.
- Pizzicato: **683 note events**.
- Harp: **443 note events**.
- Mallet: **236 note events**, first entering at beat 32 = **bar 9**.
- Lead: **73 note events**, first entering at beat 48 = **bar 13**.
- Strings: only **18 note events** in the entire reconstruction; they are mostly long octave doublings.
- Background synth: **94 note events**, first entering at beat 128 = **bar 33**.

These counts matter because the parts have radically different jobs. The atmosphere is not created by every track playing dense harmony. Some layers are highly active while others are almost empty.

---

# 1. Core emotional reading

The second-half reconstruction does not behave like ordinary “sad minor” music.

Best descriptors:

- enchanted
- atmospheric
- melancholy
- mysterious
- subtly uncanny
- beautiful but slightly abnormal
- environmental rather than narrative

The strongest summary is:

> **A familiar place becomes subtly strange, then feels larger when it becomes familiar again.**

The unease is not produced by horror sound design. It comes from a temporary change in pitch language while tempo, instrumentation, pulse, and general dynamics remain recognizable.

---

# 2. The home world is D Dorian, not generic D minor

The stable pitch collection in the home sections is overwhelmingly:

**D – E – F – G – A – B – C**

That is **D Dorian**.

The emotionally important contradiction is:

- **F natural** = minor 3rd, giving D its melancholy/minor identity.
- **B natural** = natural 6th, giving the minor world light and openness.

If the mode were ordinary D natural minor, the sixth would be **Bb**. The presence of **B natural** keeps the music from settling into conventional gloom.

Reusable principle:

> **Minor 3rd + natural 6th is an excellent way to create melancholy with a built-in light source.**

This is one reason the home sections can feel enchanted rather than tragic.

---

# 3. The opening is more “harmonic field” than chord progression

For a long stretch, **D functions as the ground** while the upper notes rotate through different extensions.

A useful reduction of the opening behavior is approximately:

- **Dm13-ish:** D–F–A–C–E–B
- **Dm11-ish:** D–F–A–C–G
- **Dm13 color:** D–F–A–C–B
- **Dm9:** D–F–A–C–E

The important point is not the exact jazz label. The important point is that the root/tonal floor changes much less than the upper color.

Effect:

- D feels like a location.
- E, G, B, and other extensions change the lighting inside that location.
- Harmony feels environmental rather than progression-driven.

Reusable principle:

> **To make harmony feel like a place, keep the tonal ground stable and move extensions around it.**

---

# 4. Extensions are used as individual emotional voices

The reconstruction repeatedly makes specific extension tones audible rather than burying them inside giant block chords.

Important examples:

- **E over D** = 9th; unresolved openness.
- **G over D** = 11th; suspended/modal openness.
- **B over D** = natural 13th; Dorian brightness.

This is especially clear in the strings. The strings often hold one octave-doubled pitch for two bars rather than playing a complete chord.

That means the listener hears the extension as an emotional object.

Reusable principle:

> **A single sustained extension can color a harmony more clearly than a full pad chord.**

---

# 5. Instrument roles are sharply differentiated

## Pizzicato — harmonic/rhythmic engine

The pizzicato carries the densest note activity and much of the harmonic pulse. It repeatedly combines low D-centered material with upper chord tones and extensions.

It creates:

- pulse without drums
- harmonic continuity
- physical movement
- a sense of small mechanisms operating inside a larger environment

## Harp — connective ornament

The harp is also active, but less structurally dominant than the pizzicato. It fills gaps, answers figures, and creates connective motion.

## Mallet — bright detail / recognizable attack

The mallet enters at **bar 9**, after the basic world has already been established.

Its attack makes new rhythmic/melodic detail immediately legible.

## Main lead — foreground event

The main lead waits until **bar 13**. That delay is crucial: the listener learns the environment before receiving the large foreground statement.

## Strings — emotional lighting

Only **18 total note events**. Mostly long octave notes.

The strings do not continuously supply harmony. They selectively magnify important scale degrees.

## Background synth — transformed homecoming

The background synth waits until **bar 33**, after the chromatic excursion. A brand-new timbre arrives at the moment the familiar pitch world returns.

This creates the feeling:

> **Home returned, but the listener's experience of home changed.**

---

# 6. Arrangement architecture

A practical structural reading of the 51-bar reconstruction:

### Bars 1–8 — establish the Dorian environment

Pizzicato, harp, and sparse long strings teach the tonal world.

### Bars 9–12 — add mallet detail

The environment becomes more active without yet revealing the main lead.

### Bars 13–16 — first large lead statement

The main lead becomes foreground only after the harmonic world is familiar.

### Bars 17–18 — reduction to a simpler Dm7-like skeleton

Pitch collection is essentially **D–F–A–C**. Extensions temporarily disappear.

Function: reset the ear before the chromatic excursion.

### Bars 19–30 — chromatic / uncanny region

The pitch language introduces **Ab and Eb** and repeatedly cycles through an altered G-centered area and an F-minor area.

### Bars 31–32 — reduction to Fm7-like skeleton

The strange region is simplified before the return.

### Bars 33 onward — D Dorian returns

The familiar seven-note Dorian collection returns, but now the arrangement is denser and the background synth enters.

### Final bars — decompression / interval collapse

The arrangement strips down and moves through open intervals toward a less explicit final identity.

---

# 7. The chromatic detour is controlled, not random

The stable home collection is:

**D E F G A B C**

The uncanny section adds two especially important foreign pitches:

**Ab** and **Eb**.

The repeated cell can be reduced approximately to:

## Altered G-centered sonority

**G – B – D – F – Ab – C**

Possible functional reading:

**G7(b9, add11)**

Important friction:

- G / Ab = semitone
- B / C = semitone
- B / F = tritone

## F-minor-centered response

**F – Ab – C – Eb – G – D**

An Fm13-ish color.

The transition is strange because the G altered sonority strongly suggests direction, but the music does not resolve in the most conventional way. Instead it moves into the F-minor region.

The effect is not simply “dissonant.” It is **direction without the expected destination**.

Reusable principle:

> **Unease can come from giving the listener a familiar tension signal and then answering it with a nearby but unexpected harmonic region.**

---

# 8. Why the uncanny section does not become horror

Several dimensions stay stable while harmony changes strongly:

- tempo remains the same
- instrumental family remains recognizable
- rhythmic behavior remains repetitive
- dynamics do not suddenly explode
- there is no horror-specific sound effect layer

Therefore the listener hears:

> **“Something about this place changed.”**

rather than:

> “A threat appeared.”

Reusable principle:

> **For subtle unease, change one musical dimension strongly while leaving the others familiar.**

If harmony, timbre, tempo, dynamics, and register all become frightening at once, the effect turns into explicit horror.

---

# 9. The C → B → C strings

This is one of the most important details in the reconstruction and a major user preference.

The strings are octave doubled and extremely sparse.

Exact timing in this MIDI reconstruction:

- **C5 + C6** begins at beat 96 = **bar 25**, held for 8 beats / two bars.
- **B4 + B5** begins at beat 104 = **bar 27**, held for 8 beats / two bars.
- **C5 + C6** begins at beat 112 = **bar 29**, held for 8 beats / two bars.

So the large background gesture is:

**C → B → C**

Every move is a semitone / half step.

Why it works:

1. The motion is tiny in pitch distance but enormous in duration.
2. Sustained strings make the listener live inside each note long enough to perceive the recoloring.
3. C and B already belong to the surrounding harmonic vocabulary; the strings are not introducing a random foreign “scary note.”
4. Against a G-centered sonority, **C** reads as suspended/11-like while **B** reads as the defining major third. The movement therefore shifts the meaning of the same environment.
5. Octave doubling increases physical size without increasing harmonic complexity.

Best reusable description:

> **A slow semitone shift in a sustained background voice can radically recolor familiar harmony without disrupting its identity.**

Another useful phrasing:

> **A new chord says “we are somewhere else.” A single long voice moving by a half step says “we are still here, but something changed.”**

This device is particularly useful for:

- nostalgia
- unease
- bittersweetness
- enchanted strangeness
- same-place/different-feeling transformations

---

# 10. The main lead uses large consonant leaps

The main lead is not primarily scalar.

Its first major phrase contains repeated jumps of fourths, fifths, sixths, and octave-displaced chord/extension tones.

The important compositional mechanism is:

- sparse attacks
- large vertical movement
- consonant relationship to the current harmonic collection
- eventual downward folding

This makes the melody feel like **points appearing at different heights** rather than a singer walking through a scale.

Reusable principle:

> **Atmospheric melody can be extremely specific. Space comes from interval design and rests, not from making the melody vague.**

---

# 11. Exact motif repetition is a feature, not a flaw

Major lead material returns later essentially unchanged.

The arrangement changes around it.

Therefore the same phrase acquires a new emotional meaning.

This is critical:

> **Do not constantly rewrite a good motif just to create “development.”**

Development can come from:

- new supporting voice
- new register around the motif
- wider stereo field
- denser accompaniment
- changed bass
- returning after an unstable section

A recognizable exact return can be more emotionally powerful than a highly altered variation.

---

# 12. Subtraction is a transition tool

Two especially important reductions occur around structural changes:

- Bars 17–18 reduce toward a **Dm7** skeleton: D–F–A–C.
- Bars 31–32 reduce toward an **Fm7** skeleton: F–Ab–C–Eb.

Rather than using flashy transition effects, the arrangement removes extensions and complexity.

This resets the listener's harmonic expectations.

Reusable principle:

> **Before a major change, remove information. The ear becomes more sensitive to whatever returns next.**

---

# 13. Homecoming is the real climax mechanism

At **bar 33** the pitch collection returns to the familiar D-Dorian world:

**D E F G A B C**

But the arrangement is now richer, and the **background synth enters for the first time**.

The emotional payoff comes from recognition plus transformation:

- familiar pitch language
- familiar motifs
- new timbre
- greater density
- wider activity

The harmony itself does not need to become more sophisticated.

Reusable principle:

> **The return of a familiar world can be a climax if the listener has spent enough time away from it.**

---

# 14. Ending mechanism: from identity back to environment

The final section increasingly favors open intervals and subtraction.

A useful reduction of the descending dyad behavior includes motions such as:

- A–E
- G–D
- F–C
- D–A

These are largely open fifths.

Later the intervals become more compact.

The effect is that the arrangement seems to **collapse inward**:

**open space → smaller intervals → reduced sonority**

Instead of ending with a strong “D minor, final answer” cadence, the piece gives up some of its explicit emotional labeling.

Reusable principle:

> **An atmospheric ending can become more powerful by removing the note that most clearly names the emotion.**

This returns the music from “statement” to “place.”

---

# 15. Most reusable composition rules from this study

1. **Establish a modal home before violating it.** If the listener does not know the rules, breaking them has little emotional meaning.
2. **Use a pedal/tonal floor to make harmony feel environmental.** Let upper extensions change around a stable ground.
3. **Use Dorian natural 6 as light inside minor melancholy.** The bright scale degree can prevent minor harmony from becoming conventionally sad.
4. **Let extension tones exist as individual voices.** A held 9th or 13th can be more emotionally legible than a giant block voicing.
5. **Give instruments different jobs.** Pizzicato can be engine, harp can be connector, strings can be lighting, lead can be event.
6. **Delay the foreground melody until the world is established—but once it arrives, make it identifiable.**
7. **Use large consonant leaps for atmospheric melody.** Atmosphere does not require vague or unmemorable melodic writing.
8. **Repeat strong motifs exactly when appropriate.** Let context create new meaning.
9. **Reserve chromaticism for one important region.** Foreign notes matter more when they are rare.
10. **For subtle unease, preserve tempo/timbre/pulse while changing pitch language.**
11. **Use slow semitone motion in sustained background voices for “same place, something changed.”**
12. **Use subtraction before structural transitions.** Removing extensions resets the ear.
13. **Make homecoming a payoff.** Familiar material after instability can feel larger without becoming louder.
14. **Introduce a new timbre on the return.** Familiar harmony + new orchestration can imply that the world is the same but the listener is not.
15. **End by removing explicit identity.** Open intervals and withheld thirds preserve atmosphere.

---

# 16. Implications for future original tracks

For an original piece inspired by the emotional logic—not the notes—of this reconstruction:

- Pick a different modal center.
- Establish one memorable melodic cell early enough that the listener can latch onto it.
- Let the main harmonic world remain stable for a substantial period.
- Use a natural-6/Dorian color or another single defining scale degree as a recognizable “light” note.
- Make the uneasy section alter only one or two scale degrees first.
- Use one long background semitone gesture, potentially something like **X → X-1 → X**, rather than copying C→B→C literally.
- Keep the same instrument family during the uneasy region so the feeling is “changed place,” not “new scene.”
- Strip complexity immediately before returning home.
- On the return, repeat the main hook exactly or nearly exactly while adding one genuinely new orchestral/timbral layer.
- Let the final section decompose into open intervals rather than resolving with a conventional final cadence.

Most important creative test:

> **If all atmosphere/effects are muted, is there still a recognizable melodic identity?**

The reference track succeeds because the environment is strong, but the motifs are also specific enough to survive outside the ambience.
